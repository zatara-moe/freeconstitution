## Contact your representatives

- You have one U.S. representative and two U.S. senators. Find them at [house.gov](https://www.house.gov/representatives/find-your-representative) and [senate.gov](https://www.senate.gov/senators/senators-contact.htm).
- Find your state and local officials at [usa.gov](https://www.usa.gov/elected-officials).
- Call, email, or use the contact form on their website.
- Say your name, your city or ZIP code, and one issue. Say what you want them to do.
- Short and personal works best. One story about how an issue affects you is enough.

## Speak at a public meeting

- City councils, county boards, and school boards hold public meetings.
- Many set aside time for public comment. You sign up, then speak for a few minutes.
- Check the agenda online first. Some meetings also take written comments.

## Comment on a federal rule

- Federal agencies must usually ask the public before they make a new rule.
- You can read proposed rules and leave a comment at [regulations.gov](https://www.regulations.gov).
- Agencies must consider the comments they get. A specific comment helps more than a general one.

## Petition and protest

- The First Amendment protects your right to petition the government and to gather peacefully.
- A petition can be a letter signed by many people, or a formal petition your state allows.
- Before a protest, read [At a protest](/situations/at-a-protest/).

## Vote and run for office

- U.S. citizens 18 and older can vote. See [First-time voter](/situations/first-time-voter/).
- The Constitution sets minimum ages for federal office: 25 for the House, 30 for the Senate, and 35 for President.
- Many local offices, like school board, have lower age limits. Check your state's rules.

## Open to everyone

- Representatives serve everyone in their district, not only voters.
- Contacting officials, public comment, and petitions are open to everyone, citizen or not.
- Noncitizens have free speech rights too. But an arrest at a protest can affect your immigration case. Read [At a protest](/situations/at-a-protest/) first.
- Voting in federal and state elections and running for federal office are for citizens only.
