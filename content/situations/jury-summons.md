## What a jury summons is

- A summons is an official order to come to court for possible jury service.
- It does not mean you are in trouble.
- Juries decide criminal cases and many civil cases. The 6th and 7th Amendments protect the right to a jury trial.
- Many people who come in are never picked for a trial.

## Who can serve

- Only U.S. citizens can serve on a jury.
- You must be 18 or older and live in the court's area.
- For federal juries, you must also read, write, and speak English.
- People charged with or convicted of a felony cannot serve on a federal jury unless their rights were restored. State rules differ.

## If you are not a U.S. citizen

- Courts often get names from DMV and voter lists, so noncitizens sometimes get a summons by mistake.
- Answer the summons and say that you are not a U.S. citizen.
- Never check a box saying you are a citizen. A false claim of citizenship can lead to deportation.

## If the dates don't work

- Most courts let you postpone once or more. Ask before the deadline.
- You can ask to be excused for a hardship, like caring for a child or a medical issue. Courts decide case by case.
- Full-time students can often move their date to a school break.

## Your job

- Federal law protects permanent employees from being fired or threatened for serving on a federal jury.
- Most states have similar laws for state juries.
- Your employer may not have to pay you while you serve. The court pays a small daily amount.
- Ask the court for a letter that shows the days you served.

## While you serve

- Don't research the case, the people, or the lawyers online.
- Don't post about the case or talk about it with anyone until it ends.
- Follow the judge's instructions. Breaking them can lead to a mistrial or a penalty.

## The law you are citing

**Sixth Amendment (1791):** A person charged with a crime has the right to a speedy, public trial by an impartial jury.

> In all criminal prosecutions, the accused shall enjoy the right to a speedy and public trial, by an impartial jury

**Seventh Amendment (1791):** Many civil cases in federal court have the right to a jury.

> In Suits at common law, where the value in controversy shall exceed twenty dollars, the right of trial by jury shall be preserved
