## What a provisional ballot is

A provisional ballot is a ballot set aside and counted later. Officials first check whether you were eligible. In most states, federal law lets you cast one if a poll worker says you cannot vote a regular ballot. A few states with same-day registration at the polls handle this differently.

## The Election Protection Hotline

Call or text 1-866-OUR-VOTE (1-866-687-8683). It is a free national hotline. Trained volunteers answer, including lawyers. They can help you while you are at the polling place.

Other languages:

- Spanish: 1-888-VE-Y-VOTA (1-888-839-8682)
- Asian languages: 1-888-API-VOTE (1-888-274-8683)
- Arabic: 1-844-YALLA-US (1-844-925-5287)

## If your name is not on the list

- Ask the poll worker to check again. Names are sometimes misspelled or listed under a different name.
- Ask if you are at the right polling place. In many states, you must vote at your assigned place.
- If you are at the wrong place, ask where yours is. Go there if you have time.
- If it is not fixed, say: "I would like to cast a provisional ballot."
- Ask how to check later whether your ballot was counted. Most states let you check online.

## If you do not have the ID they ask for

- ID rules differ by state. Some states require photo ID. Some accept other ID. Some do not ask for ID.
- If you do not have the right ID, ask for a provisional ballot.
- In some states, you can bring ID to the election office within a few days. Then your ballot can count. Ask about your state's rules.

## If you are told you are not registered

- Ask: "Does this state allow same-day registration?"
- If yes, ask what you need. This often includes proof of where you live.
- If no, ask for a provisional ballot. It may or may not count, but it creates a record that you tried.

## If someone tries to stop you from voting

- Scaring or threatening voters is illegal under federal law.
- Call the Election Protection Hotline.
- Write down the time, the place, and what the person said or did.
- You can also contact your state attorney general or the U.S. Department of Justice at 1-800-253-3931.

## Before Election Day

Check your registration ahead of time at vote.gov. Most states let you check online in about a minute. Many also let you update your address online.

## The law you are citing

**Fifteenth Amendment (1870):** States cannot block you from voting because of your race.

> The right of citizens of the United States to vote shall not be denied or abridged by the United States or by any State on account of race, color, or previous condition of servitude.

**Nineteenth Amendment (1920):** States cannot block you from voting because of your sex.

> The right of citizens of the United States to vote shall not be denied or abridged by the United States or by any State on account of sex.

**Twenty-fourth Amendment (1964):** No one can charge you a tax to vote in a federal election.

> The right of citizens of the United States to vote in any primary or other election for President or Vice President, for electors for President or Vice President, or for Senator or Representative in Congress, shall not be denied or abridged by the United States or any State by reason of failure to pay any poll tax or other tax.

**Twenty-sixth Amendment (1971):** If you are 18 or older, states cannot block you from voting because of your age.

> The right of citizens of the United States, who are eighteen years of age or older, to vote shall not be denied or abridged by the United States or by any State on account of age.
