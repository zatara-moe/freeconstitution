## Verbatim

The Ratification of the Conventions of nine States, shall be sufficient for the Establishment of this Constitution between the States so ratifying the Same.

Done in Convention by the Unanimous Consent of the States present the Seventeenth Day of September in the Year of our Lord one thousand seven hundred and Eighty seven and of the Independence of the United States of America the Twelfth. In witness whereof We have hereunto subscribed our Names.

## Plain English

Once conventions in nine states ratify this Constitution, it takes effect among those states.

The Convention finished this Constitution on September 17, 1787, with the consent of all states present. That was the twelfth year of American independence. The signers' names follow.

## About

Article VII set the rule for how the Constitution would start. It needed nine of the thirteen states, not all of them. Each state held a special convention to vote.

This was a big change. The Articles of Confederation, the earlier system, needed every state to agree to any change.

New Hampshire became the ninth state to ratify on June 21, 1788. Virginia followed four days later and New York a month after that. North Carolina ratified in November 1789, and Rhode Island in May 1790.

The date in this article, September 17, 1787, is now marked as Constitution Day. George Washington signed as President of the Convention, along with 38 other delegates from twelve states. Rhode Island sent no delegates.

"Unanimous Consent of the States present" means every state delegation voted yes. It does not mean every delegate agreed. Edmund Randolph and George Mason of Virginia and Elbridge Gerry of Massachusetts refused to sign.
