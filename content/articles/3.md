## Verbatim

**Section 1.** The judicial Power of the United States, shall be vested in one supreme Court, and in such inferior Courts as the Congress may from time to time ordain and establish. The Judges, both of the supreme and inferior Courts, shall hold their Offices during good Behaviour, and shall, at stated Times, receive for their Services, a Compensation, which shall not be diminished during their Continuance in Office.

**Section 2.** The judicial Power shall extend to all Cases, in Law and Equity, arising under this Constitution, the Laws of the United States, and Treaties made, or which shall be made, under their Authority;—to all Cases affecting Ambassadors, other public Ministers and Consuls;—to all Cases of admiralty and maritime Jurisdiction;—to Controversies to which the United States shall be a Party;—to Controversies between two or more States;—between a State and Citizens of another State;—between Citizens of different States,—between Citizens of the same State claiming Lands under Grants of different States, and between a State, or the Citizens thereof, and foreign States, Citizens or Subjects.

In all Cases affecting Ambassadors, other public Ministers and Consuls, and those in which a State shall be Party, the supreme Court shall have original Jurisdiction. In all the other Cases before mentioned, the supreme Court shall have appellate Jurisdiction, both as to Law and Fact, with such Exceptions, and under such Regulations as the Congress shall make.

The Trial of all Crimes, except in Cases of Impeachment, shall be by Jury; and such Trial shall be held in the State where the said Crimes shall have been committed; but when not committed within any State, the Trial shall be at such Place or Places as the Congress may by Law have directed.

**Section 3.** Treason against the United States, shall consist only in levying War against them, or in adhering to their Enemies, giving them Aid and Comfort. No Person shall be convicted of Treason unless on the Testimony of two Witnesses to the same overt Act, or on Confession in open Court.

The Congress shall have Power to declare the Punishment of Treason, but no Attainder of Treason shall work Corruption of Blood, or Forfeiture except during the Life of the Person attainted.

## Plain English

### Section 1 — The federal courts

The judicial power, meaning the power to decide cases, belongs to one Supreme Court. It also belongs to any lower federal courts Congress creates.

Federal judges keep their jobs "during good Behaviour." In practice, this means for life unless they resign or are impeached and removed. Their pay cannot be cut while they serve.

### Section 2 — Which cases federal courts hear

Federal courts can hear these kinds of cases:

- Cases about the Constitution, federal laws, and treaties
- Cases involving ambassadors and other foreign officials
- Cases about ships and the sea
- Cases where the United States is a party
- Disputes between two or more states
- Disputes between a state and citizens of another state
- Disputes between citizens of different states
- Disputes between citizens of the same state over land granted by different states
- Disputes between a state or its citizens and foreign countries or their citizens

*Note: The Eleventh Amendment (1795) limits lawsuits against a state by citizens of another state or another country.*

Some cases start at the Supreme Court. These include cases involving ambassadors and cases where a state is a party. This is called original jurisdiction.

In all other cases, the Supreme Court hears appeals from lower courts. This is called appellate jurisdiction. Congress can make exceptions and rules for these appeals.

All criminal trials, except impeachments, must be by jury. The trial is held in the state where the crime happened. If the crime happened outside any state, Congress decides where the trial is held.

### Section 3 — Treason

Treason against the United States means only two things. It means making war against the country, or helping its enemies.

A person can be convicted of treason only in two ways. Two witnesses must testify to the same open act, or the person must confess in open court.

Congress sets the punishment for treason. But the punishment cannot extend to the person's family. Their heirs cannot lose the right to inherit, and property can be taken only during the person's own life.

## About

Article III creates the federal courts. It is short. It leaves many details to Congress, such as how many justices sit on the Supreme Court and how the lower courts are organized. Those details come from federal laws, not the Constitution.

The Court's most important power is *judicial review*, meaning the power to strike down laws that conflict with the Constitution. Article III does not name this power. The Court claimed it in *Marbury v. Madison* (1803), and it has been accepted ever since.

Lifetime service is meant to protect judges from political pressure. The only way to remove a federal judge is impeachment. The House has impeached 15 federal judges, and the Senate has removed some of them.

The writers defined treason narrowly on purpose. In England, the Crown had used broad treason charges against political opponents. The two-witness rule makes treason hard to prove, and treason convictions have been rare.

"Corruption of blood" was an old English rule. A person convicted of treason lost the right to pass property to their family. The Constitution bans this, so the punishment stays with the person convicted.
