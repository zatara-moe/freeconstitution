Article VII: Approving the Constitution

Ratification

About 1 min Read

The one-line version

The Constitution would take effect once conventions in nine of the thirteen states ratified it.

Plain English

Once conventions in nine states ratify this Constitution, it takes effect among those states.

The Convention finished this Constitution on September 17, 1787, with the consent of all states present. That was the twelfth year of American independence. The signers' names follow.

Original text, 1788

The Ratification of the Conventions of nine States, shall be sufficient for the Establishment of this Constitution between the States so ratifying the Same.

Done in Convention by the Unanimous Consent of the States present the Seventeenth Day of September in the Year of our Lord one thousand seven hundred and Eighty seven and of the Independence of the United States of America the Twelfth. In witness whereof We have hereunto subscribed our Names.

Myth check

MythAll 13 states had to agree before the Constitution took effect.

FactOnly nine were needed. North Carolina and Rhode Island did not ratify until after the new government started.

MythEvery delegate at the Convention signed the Constitution.

FactThree delegates who stayed to the end refused to sign: Edmund Randolph, George Mason, and Elbridge Gerry.

Common questions

What does Article VII say in simple terms?

Article VII says the Constitution would take effect once conventions in nine states ratified it, and only among those states. It also records that the Convention finished the Constitution on September 17, 1787.

When was the Constitution ratified?

New Hampshire became the ninth state to ratify on June 21, 1788, which was enough for the Constitution to take effect. The last of the 13 states, Rhode Island, ratified in May 1790.

How many states had to ratify the Constitution?

Nine of the 13. Article VII set that number so one or two holdouts could not block the new system. Each state voted through a special convention.

Why is Constitution Day on September 17?

It is the day the delegates signed the Constitution in 1787. Article VII records that date. George Washington signed as President of the Convention, along with 38 other delegates from twelve states.

Go deeper

The scene · 1787

On September 17, 1787, the delegates in Philadelphia signed the finished Constitution. Then it went to the states. Each state held a special convention to vote yes or no. New Hampshire became the ninth state to say yes on June 21, 1788.

Back then

The Articles of Confederation needed all 13 states to agree to any change. Rhode Island had refused even to send delegates. The writers set the bar at nine states so one or two holdouts could not block the new system.

Who argued what

Federalists

The Articles had failed. The Constitution should be ratified now, and changes could follow by amendment.

Anti-Federalists

The Constitution gave the national government too much power and had no bill of rights. States should not ratify it as written.

More history and context

Article VII set the rule for how the Constitution would start. It needed nine of the thirteen states, not all of them. Each state held a special convention to vote.

This was a big change. The Articles of Confederation, the earlier system, needed every state to agree to any change.

New Hampshire became the ninth state to ratify on June 21, 1788. Virginia followed four days later and New York a month after that. North Carolina ratified in November 1789, and Rhode Island in May 1790.

The date in this article, September 17, 1787, is now marked as Constitution Day. George Washington signed as President of the Convention, along with 38 other delegates from twelve states. Rhode Island sent no delegates.

"Unanimous Consent of the States present" means every state delegation voted yes. It does not mean every delegate agreed. Edmund Randolph and George Mason of Virginia and Elbridge Gerry of Massachusetts refused to sign.
