I just turned 18: your civic checklist

Three things arrive at 18, voting, Selective Service, and jury duty. What each one is, who it applies to, and what to do.

About 2 min Read

The one-line version

At 18, citizens can vote and serve on juries, and most men living in the U.S. must register with Selective Service.

1. Register to vote (U.S. citizens)

  • The 26th Amendment protects the vote for citizens 18 and older.
  • Register at vote.gov. Deadlines differ by state. Some states let you register on Election Day.
  • Some states let you pre-register at 16 or 17.
  • If you are not a U.S. citizen, do not register. It can lead to deportation. See First-time voter.

2. Selective Service (most men 18 to 25)

  • Selective Service keeps a list of men who could be drafted if Congress ever brings back a draft. There is no draft now.
  • Most men ages 18 through 25 living in the U.S. must register within 30 days of turning 18.
  • This includes U.S. citizens, green card holders, and undocumented immigrants.
  • Men on a valid temporary visa, like a student or tourist visa, do not have to register.
  • Selective Service goes by sex assigned at birth. People assigned male at birth must register.
  • In many states, getting a driver's license signs you up. You can also register at sss.gov.
  • A 2025 law tells Selective Service to start signing men up automatically. The start date is not settled.
  • Until automatic sign-up starts, you must register yourself. Check sss.gov for the latest.

If you don't register: it can block most federal jobs, some state student aid, federal job training, and U.S. citizenship for immigrant men. Not registering is also a federal crime.

3. Jury duty (U.S. citizens)

  • Only citizens can serve on a jury. You may get a summons in the mail after you turn 18.
  • Answer it by its deadline, even if you can't serve.
  • If you are not a citizen, say so on the form. Never claim to be a citizen.
  • See Jury summons for what to expect.

What else changes at 18

  • If you are charged with a crime, your case usually goes to adult court.
  • You can sign contracts, like a lease or a phone plan. Read them before you sign.
  • The Constitution protects you before and after 18. Once you leave high school, school rules no longer cover you.
  • See Who the Constitution covers to learn where your rights apply.

Common questions

Do I have to register for Selective Service?

Most men ages 18 through 25 living in the U.S. must register within 30 days of turning 18. This includes green card holders and undocumented immigrants. Men on a valid temporary visa, like a student visa, do not. Automatic sign-up is planned, so check sss.gov.

What happens if I don't register for Selective Service?

It can block most federal jobs, some state student aid, federal job training, and U.S. citizenship for immigrant men. Not registering is also a federal crime.

Can I vote as soon as I turn 18?

If you are a U.S. citizen, yes. In most states you must register first. Some states let you register at 16 or 17. Deadlines differ by state. Start at vote.gov.
