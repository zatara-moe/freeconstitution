Words

Legal words in plain English. On any page, tap an underlined word to see its meaning.

ABCDEFGHIJMNPRSTUVW

A

actively contested

Our label for questions that judges and lawyers still disagree about, which the Supreme Court has not fully settled.

amendment

A formal change or addition to the Constitution. There have been 27.

See where it matters
apportionment

Dividing seats in the House of Representatives among the states based on population, as counted in the census.

See where it matters
arrest

When police take you into custody because they believe you committed a crime.

See where it matters

B

bail

Money or conditions a court sets so a person charged with a crime can stay out of jail before trial.

See where it matters
Bill of Rights

The first 10 amendments to the Constitution, ratified in 1791.

See where it matters
birthright citizenship

U.S. citizenship a person gets by being born on U.S. soil, under the 14th Amendment.

See where it matters

C

census

The official count of everyone living in the United States, taken every 10 years.

See where it matters
checks and balances

Ways each branch of government can limit the others, such as the veto, Senate approval of judges, and judicial review.

See where it matters
citizen

A legal member of the nation. The 14th Amendment makes people born or naturalized in the U.S., and subject to its jurisdiction, citizens.

See where it matters
commerce clause

The part of Article I that lets Congress regulate trade with other countries, among the states, and with Native American tribes.

See where it matters
Congress

The lawmaking branch of the federal government. It has two parts: the Senate and the House of Representatives.

See where it matters
consent to a search

Agreeing to let police search you or your things. If you agree, police may not need a warrant. You can say no.

See where it matters
cruel and unusual punishment

Punishment the 8th Amendment forbids, such as punishment courts find barbaric or far out of proportion to the crime.

See where it matters
custody

Being under arrest, or held so that a reasonable person would not feel free to leave. It matters for Miranda warnings.

See where it matters

D

detained

Held by police for a period of time, so you are not free to leave, even if you are not under arrest.

See where it matters
double jeopardy

Being tried or punished twice for the same crime by the same government. The 5th Amendment forbids it.

See where it matters
due process

Fair procedures the government must follow before it takes away your life, liberty, or property.

See where it matters

E

Electoral College

The system that chooses the president. Each state gets electors equal to its number of senators plus House members.

See where it matters
eminent domain

The government's power to take private property for public use. The 5th Amendment requires it to pay a fair price.

See where it matters
enumerated powers

Powers the Constitution lists in writing for the federal government, mostly for Congress in Article I, Section 8.

See where it matters
equal protection

The 14th Amendment rule that states must treat people equally under the law.

See where it matters
establishment clause

The First Amendment rule that the government cannot set up an official religion. Courts debate exactly how far it reaches.

See where it matters
executive order

A written directive from the president telling federal agencies what to do. Courts can strike one down if it conflicts with the Constitution or law.

See where it matters

F

federalism

The sharing of power between the national government and the state governments.

See where it matters
free exercise clause

The First Amendment protection of your right to practice your religion, or no religion, as you choose.

See where it matters
freedom of assembly

Your First Amendment right to gather peacefully with others, such as at a protest or a meeting.

See where it matters
freedom of the press

The First Amendment right to publish news and opinions without government censorship.

See where it matters

G

grand jury

A group of citizens who review evidence in private and decide whether there is enough to charge someone with a serious federal crime.

See where it matters

H

House of Representatives

The part of Congress where states get seats based on population. Members serve two-year terms.

See where it matters

I

impeachment

When the House formally charges a federal official with serious wrongdoing. The Senate then holds a trial to decide on removal.

See where it matters
incorporation

How the Supreme Court has applied most Bill of Rights protections to state and local governments through the 14th Amendment.

See where it matters

J

judicial review

The power of courts to strike down laws or government actions that conflict with the Constitution.

See where it matters
jury

A group of ordinary citizens chosen to hear the evidence at a trial and decide the facts.

See where it matters
just compensation

Fair payment, usually the market value, that the government must give when it takes private property.

See where it matters

M

militia

A group of citizens trained to serve as soldiers when needed, instead of a full-time army.

See where it matters
Miranda warnings

Warnings police must give before questioning someone in custody: right to stay silent, that statements can be used, right to a lawyer.

See where it matters

N

naturalization

The legal process for a person born in another country to become a U.S. citizen.

See where it matters
necessary and proper clause

The part of Article I that lets Congress make laws needed to carry out its listed powers.

See where it matters

P

poll tax

A fee charged to vote. The 24th Amendment bans it in federal elections, and a 1966 Court ruling bans it in state elections.

See where it matters
precedent

An earlier court decision that courts follow when deciding similar cases later.

See where it matters
probable cause

Good reason, based on facts, to believe a crime happened or evidence is in a place.

See where it matters
provisional ballot

A ballot you cast when officials question whether you can vote. They check it later before deciding to count it.

See where it matters
public defender

A lawyer paid by the government to represent people charged with crimes who cannot afford a lawyer.

See where it matters

R

ratify

To formally approve. An amendment becomes part of the Constitution when three-fourths of the states approve it.

See where it matters
reasonable suspicion

A specific, fact-based reason for police to think crime may be happening. It allows a brief stop, not an arrest.

See where it matters
reserved powers

Powers not given to the federal government, which the 10th Amendment keeps for the states or the people.

See where it matters
right to petition

Your First Amendment right to ask the government to fix a problem or change a policy without being punished for asking.

See where it matters

S

search and seizure

When the government looks through you or your things (search), or takes property or a person (seizure). The 4th Amendment limits both.

See where it matters
self-incrimination

Being forced to give testimony that could be used against you in a criminal case. The 5th Amendment protects you from it.

See where it matters
Senate

The part of Congress with two senators from each state. Senators serve six-year terms.

See where it matters
separation of powers

Dividing government power among three branches: Congress makes laws, the president carries them out, and courts interpret them.

See where it matters
suffrage

The right to vote.

See where it matters
supremacy clause

The part of Article VI saying the Constitution, federal laws made under it, and treaties override conflicting state laws.

See where it matters
Supreme Court

The highest court in the United States. Its decisions about the Constitution bind all other courts.

See where it matters

T

treason

Making war against the United States or helping its enemies. The Constitution defines it narrowly and requires strong proof.

See where it matters

U

unenumerated rights

Rights not listed in the Constitution that people still keep. The 9th Amendment says listing some rights does not deny others.

See where it matters

V

veto

The president's power to reject a bill. Congress can override a veto with a two-thirds vote in both houses.

See where it matters

W

warrant

A written order signed by a judge that allows police to search a place or arrest a person.

See where it matters
writ of habeas corpus

A court order requiring the government to explain why it is holding someone. It protects against unlawful imprisonment.
